Deskitty · behaviour spec

What she does, and when she does it.

Eleven sprites exist. This is the plan for wiring them to a day — which state runs by default, what interrupts it, how long each one holds, and what she falls back to. Every cat below is a real render from cat.blend.

640 × 512 · bottom-centre anchor side = locomotion · front = personality 11 sprites built · 5 to go

The day

Seven beats, in order. Steps 2–5 loop; 1 and 7 are the bookends.

01

First light

trigger: first unlock after 04:00 · once per day

She stretches awake, then sits and delivers the morning line — what's on today, pulled from the schedule. This is the one moment she speaks unprompted, which is exactly why it must fire once and never twice.

holds ~6s then standby resets at 01:00
02

Standby

default · no signal crossing a threshold

Her resting behaviour, and where she spends most of the day. Sit for 8–20 seconds, glance around, stand, walk a short distance along the dock, sit again. The randomised dwell is what stops her reading as a looping GIF.

dwell 8–20s random walks 60–200px any signal interrupts
03

The work piles up

signal: task load crosses a level boundary

Fatness is not a state — it's a dial that applies to whatever she's already doing. Cross a threshold and she inflates a level, then carries on with standby at the new weight. In profile this has to read as belly depth, since width is invisible from the side.

levels 0 → 3 eases over 1.5s deflates as tasks close
04

The machine strains

signal: sustained CPU, thermal, or memory pressure

The one true interrupt. She hunches small, flattens her ears and tucks her tail — flattened ears are the most readable stress cue in the whole set, which is why the ears need their own bones. Holds, then decays rather than snapping back.

needs >8s sustained holds 12s decays over 4s
05

All clear

signal: zero tasks AND system quiet for 2 min

Nothing queued, nothing hot. Sunglasses, headphones, a nod to a beat you can't hear. The empty state has to be the best-looking one she has — it's what you see when everything is fine, and "fine" shouldn't look like "off".

any task exits instantly lowest priority
06

You leave

signal: no keyboard or mouse for 10 min

Curls up and breathes. Wakes on the first input event, not on a timer — she should already be sitting up by the time you've focused a window.

wake on first input skips stretch
07

Late

clock: past 01:00 and still working

Sleep becomes sticky — she settles faster and stays down longer. The only beat driven by the clock rather than a machine signal, and the only one that's a comment on you rather than on the computer. Keep it wordless.

idle threshold 10m → 3m arms step 01

Rules that keep her sane

Four constraints. Skip these and she reads as a screensaver.

Rule 01

Fire on crossings, not on ticks

React when a signal crosses a threshold, never on every poll. A pet that re-announces the same 71% CPU every two seconds is noise, and noise gets quit.

Rule 02

Memory pressure needs a hold timer

The kernel reports pressure arriving but not clearing. Without a decay timer she gets stuck permanently frightened — a bug worth writing down before it's lived through.

Rule 03

One reaction per 90 seconds

A global cooldown across all interrupts. Whatever the machine is doing, she comments at most once a minute and a half.

Rule 04

Everything returns to standby

Priority when signals collide: stress > sleep > chill > standby. Fatness sits outside the ladder — it multiplies with whatever wins.

The cost hiding in step 03

Because fatness applies to every state rather than being one of them, sprites multiply. Four weight levels across eight states is not eight renders — it's thirty-two, plus eight walk frames at each weight.

Three ways out, in order of how much they cost you: apply fatness to standby poses only and let the rest stay neutral; cut to two weight levels instead of four; or render live in 3D and make weight a continuous float. The third is the only one that matches what the idea actually describes, and it's also the one that burns CPU in an app built to watch your CPU.

8 states × 4 weights + 8 walk × 4 = 64 sprites
