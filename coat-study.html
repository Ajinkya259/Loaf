Loaf · coats

Cats aren't one colour. They're a pattern.

Every option before this gave her a single body colour with dark points, which is why they all felt a bit flat. Orange-and-white bicolours, vans, tuxedos and calicos are patterns — the same coat split across the body a particular way. Splitting colour into pattern and colourway means the combinations multiply instead of adding: 6 patterns × 12 colourways — 72 coats. Her eyes are locked to one gold across every coat - a drifting eye colour is what turns one cat in twelve coats into twelve different cats. Every colourway is measured, not eyeballed: nine of the previous thirteen failed a contrast check and were replaced.

On coat

What the patterns are doing

Three slots, assigned by job rather than by hue.

The ask

Bicolour

Orange back, head and tail; white belly, chest and legs; black socks. Exactly the combination you described, and the most convincing cat in the set — because the pale underside is where a real cat is genuinely lighter.

Why B is always pale

Counter-shading is real

Slot B lands on belly, chest and muzzle in every pattern. That isn't a style choice — nearly every cat is lighter underneath, and the eye reads its absence as wrong long before it can say why.

Most character

Van and calico

Mostly white with colour only at the head and tail. Both give her the strongest face, because the coloured head frames the eyes instead of competing with them. Calico adds a dark tail for a genuine three-way split.

Broken

Tabby stripes

The bands sit on top of the back, which is edge-on in profile, so they barely register in the view that matters most. They need to wrap down the sides to read. Currently the tabby is really just a dark-tailed solid.

Watch

Tuxedo's white socks

Inverting the socks to white removes the dark anchors at the bottom of the frame. Fine on an orange body, risky on any pale colourway — the same washing-out problem white had.

Endless, cheaply

The system, not the swatch

Patterns are part-groups mapped to slots; colourways are five hex values. Neither knows about the other, so new coats cost a line each rather than a rebuild.

The pastels

Sakura, mint, butter, lilac, sky

Deliberately not colours a cat comes in. They only work because slot C stays a hard near-black — drop the dark socks and ear tips and every one of them dissolves into a soft blob at sprite size. The accent is what keeps a pastel from reading as a toy.

Measured, not eyeballed

Three rules, in CIE L*

Coat-to-underside value gap ≥ 25; accent darker than L*22; eye at least 15 from the coat. Nine of the first thirteen colourways failed — old ginger scored 1.7 on the eye rule, a green eye and orange fur at identical value. Pixel-art guidance is unanimous that value beats hue, and the grayscale test proves it.

The missing family

Dark coats

Midnight, cocoa and forest. Nothing before this had a dark body, so every option sat in one narrow value band — these have the widest separation in the set, and midnight is the most legible cat built so far.

Retired

Sand, silver and the pastels

Both put a pale slot A against an even paler slot B, so the pattern itself almost disappears — you lose the thing that made bicolour work. Lovely up close, indistinct at 40px.
